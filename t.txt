Revolution vs. War: They are distinct historical events
War Debt: Britain accrued 150 million pounds in debt from the Seven Years' War
Taxation: Britain taxed colonies to help pay for war costs
Representation: Colonists were primarily angry over their lack of parliamentary representation
Sugar Act: Aimed to stop smuggling and enforce trade rules
Stamp Act: Taxed all printed materials, angering lawyers and printers
Boycotts: Colonists used economic pressure as a coordinated protest
Sons of Liberty: Organized direct, sometimes violent, actions
Declaratory Act: Parliament asserted its right to tax the colonies
Townshend Acts: New taxes on imports and a board to stop smuggling
Daughters of Liberty: Encouraged the use of homemade goods
Boston Massacre: Five colonists killed during a protest
Tea Act: Led to cheaper tea but set a dangerous tax precedent
Boston Tea Party: Protest resulting in massive tea destruction
Intolerable Acts: Punitive measures imposed by Britain
First Continental Congress: First colony-wide government resistance
Lexington/Concord: The first shots of the Revolutionary War
Bunker Hill: Early major battle with heavy British casualties
Olive Branch Petition: Last attempt at reconciliation with the King
Common Sense: Thomas Paine’s influential pamphlet pushed for independence
